# Pipeline V2 — idea in, film out

One command takes an idea, a target style and a target duration, and produces a film slice of that duration plus the pass journal: every plan, every prompt, every reasoning call, every QC verdict, every regeneration, every error. Fully automatic. No stops. No escape hatches. The creator gives feedback only after a pass has completed, and that feedback calibrates the next passes.

This is the plan. It came out of a design panel (four architectures, three judges, one synthesis) grounded in research on VLM judging of generated video and a file-by-file read of what the current code does headless — then amended by the creator's constitution below.

## The constitution of a pass

1. **A pass always completes.** Once intake passes, nothing waits on a person and nothing halts for a violation. Faults retry within budget. Violations are regenerated within budget. When a budget is exhausted the pass still assembles the film from the best-scoring candidates it has, and the journal names every guarantee that was met and every one that was not. The old law "produced or halted at a named node" becomes "produced, with every shortfall named."
2. **No escape hatches.** Every decision is a policy rule with a statement, a check, a test and provenance. No flag has a default; a missing value refuses at intake, by name, before any money.
3. **Shots are planned around subjects and the forces of antagonism.** Every shot names who is in frame, what force opposes them in that shot and how it is visible, and what changes from the shot's first frame to its last.
4. **The harness decides what reaches the video model.** Cheap candidates first, selection by measurement and rubric, then video. A shot is promoted to video generation because it won, not because it was next.
5. **QC is automatic and it regenerates.** Obvious artifacts are detected and the shot is re-generated within its attempt budget; the best candidate is promoted and the rest are journaled.
6. **Everything is journaled, and nothing can proceed without it.** A pass is its journal: `runs/<runId>/journal.ndjson` plus `steps/` and `media/`. If it is not in the journal it did not happen. Every decision is a checkpoint — a journal entry naming what was decided, from what evidence, by which rule — because the journal is what self-improvement reads. Structurally: every stage function, node and tool in the pass takes the journal as a required argument and throws by name at entry when it is missing; a caller cannot skip journaling any more than it can skip the wire.
7. **The film is rendered as images before it is rendered as video.** The reasoner plans at every level — film, scene, shot, detail — to discover every asset the film needs, deduplicates them into one bible, and the harness generates a library of at least the policy's minimum images upfront: character and prop sheets on neutral backgrounds, location coverage, and a start frame per shot. Every image is QC'd; the harness then decides which references each shot receives.

## The film loop — the engine

`bravo film` runs one loop, in one file, `agents/film.js`:

1. **Idea → shots.** One reasoner call plans the film: the references it needs (character sheets, location wides, props) and N shots of 20–30 seconds each with subject, force, change, setup and a full prompt. The harness checks the plan against the law (shot count, lengths, the sum, every field) and re-asks with the problems quoted. Journaled: the prompt, the answer, the problems.
2. **References → assets.** One still per reference (Seedream), QC'd, registered as an asset id. Every render from here on carries them as `image_asset_id` references. Journaled per still.
3. **Shots → videos.** Every shot renders in parallel as one take (prompt + reference assets). Each take is registered as a video asset. Journaled: intent, result, cost, media.
4. **QC.** Every take is measured and judged by the rubrics. Journaled per take.
5. **Planning.** One judgment call per round over every take that did not pass: `keep`, `edit` with the exact instruction, or `regenerate` with a corrected prompt — with a reason, seeing the whole film. Journaled.
6. **Editing / shots.** An edit sends the take's **video asset id** plus the instruction plus the reference assets (duration and ratio lock to the source); a regenerate renders fresh. Back to 4, up to the shot's attempt budget. Journaled as regenerations with cause and instruction; the replaced take coded as waste.
7. **Ship.** The best take per shot (a named shortfall when none passed, a named absence when none rendered), stitched, measured against the timeline gate, reported.

Faults: a transient fault retries with backoff within the fault budget; a provider refusal is journaled with the provider's words and the prompt is rewritten to clear it. Nothing stops after intake. Plates, keyframe candidates and the chain-parallel schedule remain as `--mode staged`.

### The five rules

The engine judges by exactly five rules, in `rules/film.json`, and refuses to run with more or fewer:

| id | rule | how it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| FILM-001 | It runs to length | measured: every take at least its planned seconds and within the overshoot allowance, 24 fps; the film within the scaled tolerance |
| FILM-002 | The image is clean | measured: no frozen or black segments (frame hashes and luminance); asked: no broken anatomy, morphing, garbled writing, unplanned strangers |
| FILM-003 | Something changes | asked: the state at the end differs from the start as the plan declared |
| FILM-004 | The opposition is on screen | asked: the named force is visibly acting against the subject |
| FILM-005 | People and places hold | asked: the subject matches its reference; the place is the same place, against the reference assets |

One QC call per take answers the four questions yes or no with the frame that shows it and one sentence; every answer is journaled with the frames it saw. A failing rule sends the take to the round's judgment. The shot window — how many shots, how long each — is policy (`policy.shots`), not a rule. The older books (`cinematic.json`, `screenwriting.json`, `metrics.json`, `policy.json` rules) remain for the browser director and `--mode staged`.

## Video extension — `bravo extend`

The simplest way in. One prompt — "Let's make a story about …, one or two sentences of detail" — and a target length; nothing else. The pass plans 4–6 shots of 20–30 seconds (the count follows from the target), renders shot one from its prompt, and renders every later shot as an **extension of the previous shot's footage**: the shipped take's video asset id is the reference video for the next generation, so the film continues from its own output. No reference stills, no human. QC judges every take by the five rules; a failing take is judged and edited from its own asset or regenerated within five attempts; the best ships and the chain continues from it. Stitch, timeline gate, report. Defaults are declared and journaled: 120 seconds, the default look, policy and prices.

```
bravo extend --idea "<the story>" [--seconds 120] [--style looks/default.json] [--policy policy/default.json] [--prices policy/prices.json] --server <url>
```

## The contract

```
bravo film --idea <text|file> --style <style.json> --seconds N --policy <policy.json> --prices <prices.json> [--mode edit|staged]
```

In: the idea, the style file (look, constraints, references, audio on/off, wire format, house doctrine), the target seconds, the policy file (every ceiling, count, concurrency and threshold the pass may use).

Out, under `runs/<runId>/`:

- `media/slice-a<admission>.mp4` — the film, the durable product, named by the assemble node's admission so a re-admitted assemble writes beside the earlier one; plus every plate, every keyframe candidate, every take attempt, every recorded last frame
- `journal.ndjson` + `steps/NNNN-<kind>.json` — the pass journal, in order: intake, ideation, every model call with full prompts and answers, every plan and gate report, every candidate score, every selection with its reason, every render request and result, every QC verdict with the frames it saw, every regeneration with its cause, every reservation and cost, the final assembly and its measurements
- `project.json` — the persisted state: brief, screenplay, plan, iteration records
- `cost.ndjson` — one row per paid or metered call (plates, keyframe candidates, takes, judge and reason calls): kept, wasted (coded), refused (coded); a spent row names the `reservationId` it spent from, which is how POL-003 is checked from the journal alone
- `errors.ndjson` — one typed row per detected error, the knowledge database's source of truth
- `report.md` — the pass in human terms: what was made, what was regenerated and why, which guarantees held, what the judge could not decide

## The architecture

The three-class law survives — plan and measure rules are machine-checked, judgment rules are learned — and gains a policy book, a candidate stage, and a QC loop.

| # | Stage | Decider | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | intake | policy | refuses by name anything the pass cannot run without: model slots, the audio judge slot when audio is on, TOS, a dated price file, a schema-valid style and policy; pins every hash on the pass |
| 1 | ideate → brief | text reasoner proposes, harness gates | derives the complete brief from idea + style — logline, cast, locations, dramatis (protagonist, want, opposition) — every derived field marked derived with its reason; cast + locations capped by policy |
| 2 | screenplay | existing tool gates | scenes, beats, turns, sides, antagonism on screen — SCR-001..005 exactly as today |

| 4 | policy approval | policy | the approve record with the arithmetic — plates + keyframe candidates + takes + attempt pools, and judge calls as judged items times the judge protocol's calls per item — against every ceiling; refused with the numbers before any money |
| 5 | discover | text reasoner proposes at four levels, harness dedupes and gates | film level: every entity the story needs — characters, creatures, props, wardrobe, vehicles, locations, motifs; scene level: each entity's state per scene (condition, lighting, dressing); shot level: exactly what is in frame per shot; detail level: the small visual facts that must hold; then a summary pass that merges duplicates into one bible with canonical ids, and gates: no two assets share a canonical name, every shot's assets exist, every cast member and location from the brief is an asset |

| 7 | reference selection | harness | for every shot, from the shot's assets, the best QC'd views: the subject's sheet views first, the location view whose coverage matches the declared setup, the props in hand, capped by the model's reference limit; the selection and its reason are journaled per shot |

| 10 | shoot + measure | harness | video from the promoted keyframe as the first frame (continuous joins chain the previous take's last frame instead); chain-parallel; every paid call preceded by a reservation and a journaled intent; measure gates as today (duration, fps, overshoot), CIN-003 and CIN-012 recorded on every join |
| 11 | take QC | harness + VLM | deterministic: frozen segments (motion), black or blown frames (luminance), duration and fps; rubric: morphing, extra or missing limbs, text garbage, subject swapped, setup lost, force not visible, change not visible; a failing take regenerates within the per-shot attempt budget (same keyframe first, then a new candidate); the best take by score is promoted |
| 12 | join QC | harness + VLM | dHash decides whether a cut exists — the VLM is handed the boundary and never asked to find it; the window rubric asks about action, continuity across continuous joins, screen direction |
| 13 | assemble + film QC | harness + VLM | stitch, CIN-008 on the timeline, the film rubric: each beat lands in its shot, the last beat answers the first, cast and look hold across cuts |
| 14 | complete + report | harness | the film, the journal closed, `report.md`, `errors.ndjson`; iteration record appended |
| 15 | learn | harness ETL + policy | fold into the knowledge index; the five lanes; calibration and promotion by policy criteria, automatically |

## The mechanisms

### The policy book

`rules/policy.json` — class `policy`, appliesTo `run` — validated by the same loader that refuses a blocking rule without a check. Every decision a person used to make is a rule with a check and a test: elicitation (ideation derives, never asks), approval (arithmetic), candidate counts, selection floors, QC consequences, attempt budgets per plate, per shot and per take, regeneration order, fault retry counts and backoff, completion when a budget is exhausted, promotion criteria for judge authority, recurrence criteria for rule growth. The values live in the creator's policy file; its hash rides on every record.

### The film as images first

Video is expensive, slow and the least controllable step; images are cheap, fast and parallel. So the pass renders the film as images before it renders a frame of video. The reasoner discovers assets at four levels — the film's inventory, each scene's states, each shot's frame contents, the details that must hold — and a summary pass folds those findings into one deduplicated bible: canonical ids, kinds (character, creature, prop, wardrobe, vehicle, location, motif), descriptions, states, and a shot-to-assets map. Deduplication is decided twice: the reasoner merges what it recognizes as the same thing and journals each merge with its reason; the harness then gates canonical-name uniqueness and coverage, so a duplicate cannot survive by wording.

The library follows the bible. Character sheets on a neutral background give the video model an identity it can hold across cuts; location coverage gives it a place it can hold across setups; prop and creature sheets give it the things the story handles. Counts per kind come from the policy file, and a pass must generate at least the policy's minimum library size — typically a hundred or more images for a 20–30-shot film — or name the shortfall. Every image is QC'd (matches its description, neutral background where required, no artifacts, consistent with the asset's primary view by perceptual hash) and regenerated within its attempt budget.

Then the harness decides, per shot, which references the shot receives: the subject's sheet views, the location view whose coverage matches the declared setup, the props in hand — capped by the model's reference limit, ranked by QC score, journaled with the reason. Those references feed the start frame, and the start frame feeds the video.

### Candidates before video

Video is the expensive, slow, least controllable step. Stills are cheap, fast and parallel. So every shot is storyboarded first: K keyframe candidates generated from the composed prompt with the plates as references, scored, and the winner promoted as the video's first frame. This is what "the harness decides what shots to keep" means in practice: the decision is made on evidence at image cost, and the video model receives a frame that already contains the right subject, the right setup and the right force. It also settles identity across cuts by construction — every keyframe descends from the same plates — while continuous joins keep chaining last frames. The API constraint that a first frame cannot ride with reference images is respected: cut shots carry the keyframe, continuous shots carry the previous last frame, neither carries references.

### The QC judge

The VLM never scores, never finds cuts, never counts people, never reads the line, never sets a threshold. It answers fixed rubric questions — binary or multiple-choice, each naming the rule it serves, anchored to the shot's subject, force, change, setup, plate and the style line — about frames the harness extracted at a pinned, recorded frame rate, in a recorded order. The research behind this: rubric questions anchored to the script reach human agreement around 0.85 where zero-shot scores reach 0.6; frontier models are poor scalar graders of generated video; VLMs barely perceive cuts, so cuts stay deterministic; artifacts — the obvious ones — are the class VLMs perceive best.

Defenses, each journaled as provenance: both question orders, a second frame offset on any failure, a falsification re-ask (the judge points at the frame), a contradiction check against measurement (a judge that contradicts ffprobe or dHash produces a judge-reliability row, never a film error), and a blind control on a policy-declared fraction of shots — on those shots the judge is handed the harness facts but not the plan's description, in every judgment of that shot, and the journal names the shot as blind; the calibration lane compares the judge's agreement with the creator's notes on blind and described shots. Dialogue is heard only by the audio-capable judge slot, required when audio is on.

Authority is policy, not signature, and it is keyed by rubric id: `policy.judge.spendingRubrics` lists the question ids that spend, nothing else does, and no scope name stands in for a list. The artifact rubric spends from day one — a take with an obvious artifact is regenerated, bounded by the per-shot attempt budget, because a bad take costs more than a re-roll and artifacts are what the judge sees most reliably. Story rubrics (force visible, change visible, beat landed) begin as witness — they rank candidates and file notes but do not trigger regeneration — and the policy promotes each one to spending automatically the moment its recorded agreement with the creator's post-pass notes clears the policy's precision on the policy's minimum instances. The promotion is a journaled event carrying the calibration rows that justified it. No command, no signature, no stop.

### The chain-parallel scheduler

The same node ids and postconditions as today, driven by a scheduler instead of a sequential walk. Plates depend on nothing. Keyframe candidates depend on plates. Selection depends on a shot's candidates. A cut shot's video depends on its promoted keyframe. A continuous shot depends on the previous shoot's recorded last frame. Measure and take QC depend on shoot; join QC on both neighbors; assemble on every promoted take on disk. The unit of parallelism is a chain — a cut-headed run of continuous joins, bounded by a policy length — ordered longest first. Concurrency caps for stills, chains and judge calls come from the policy file.

`reserve({nodeId, kind, units, justification})` must succeed before any paid call — a take reserves its shot's seconds, never a count of one, so the video-second and usd ceilings refuse where the approval said they would; it throws without a plan node id and refuses with a code when a ceiling would be crossed. Reasoning and judge calls reserve through the pass's reserving client: a call that names `nodeId` is reserved under that plan node (every `judge()` call does), and a planning call without one is reserved under the stage it serves (`PLANNING_STAGES`: `ideate`, `brief`, `screenplay`, `breakdown`); the justification records both. Outside those four stages a call without a node id is refused (`E-RESERVE-NO-NODE`): nothing is reserved under `schedule`, a join label or any other stage name, so join QC reserves under `chain:<shot>` and film QC under `final` by naming them on every call, and the pass makes no reservation a call does not spend. The approval counts judge calls the way the reserving client meets them: every judged item — each plate attempt, each keyframe candidate, each take attempt, each join, each film segment and the film summary — times `JUDGE_PROTOCOL.callsPerItem` (`base` 2: both question orders; `max` 5: both orders at both offsets plus the falsification re-ask), so the approval's `judgeCalls.max` is the ceiling the reservations can reach and its `base` the floor; `filmSegmentsOf` packs the shots' frames under `policy.judge.maxFramesPerCall` exactly as `filmQC` flushes them. A regenerated shot invalidates every continuous successor rendered from its previous last frame; the invalidation set is computed from joins, so a stale join measurement cannot survive.

Every unit is coded `kept` when it is rendered and re-coded `wasted` by an appended row the moment the pass knows it is not in the film; the ledger is append-only, so a take's kept row stays and the wasted row names why. The codes: `E-WASTE-INVALIDATED-SUCCESSOR` for a continuous successor's take discarded because its predecessor regenerated or promoted a take other than its last (finished or in flight), written at that regeneration and listed under its `wasted`; `E-WASTE-KEYFRAME-REJECTED` for the promoted keyframe candidate a new-candidate regeneration retires, written at the regeneration; `E-WASTE-GATE-REGENERATED` for a take a measure gate re-shot, written at the regeneration in the approved-card flow; `E-WASTE-QC-REGENERATED` for a take rejected by take QC once a later take of the shot passes; `E-WASTE-NOT-PROMOTED` for every take that lost to the promoted best on exhaustion; `E-WASTE-RESUME-DROPPED` for a take resume could not restore. In the policy flow a shot's rejected takes are coded when its QC concludes, because a rejected take can still be promoted as the best on exhaustion and a promoted take is never waste. Each take is coded once: the schedule records every take it invalidated on the shoot node (`invalidated`, beside the keyframe node's `rejected`), the QC node skips those and the takes already carrying a `waste` code in the ledger, and stamps the code on the ledger entry it codes, so a QC that concludes again after a predecessor regenerated does not recode the shot's earlier takes. Keyframe candidates that lost selection stay `kept`: the storyboard buys K candidates to choose among, and the losers are journaled with their scores in the `selection` step.

### Completion when budget runs out

The pass never stops for a violation. When a shot exhausts its attempts, the best candidate by score is promoted and the shortfall is a named error with the scores of every attempt. When the render ceiling is hit mid-pass, the remaining shots are promoted from their best keyframe candidates as stills held for their planned seconds (a journaled downgrade, coded, never silent) so the timeline is complete and measurable, and the film is assembled. `report.md` opens with the list of guarantees that did not hold. A pass that could not begin — a missing slot, a bad policy file — is refused at intake, before a single paid call, which is the only refusal the pipeline knows.

The ceiling is met at the reservation, never at the wire. `plate` and `shoot` await their reservation before the intent is journaled; a refusal is a rejected promise the node observes. A plate refusal is a fault. A shoot refusal whose code begins `E-BUDGET-` in the policy flow is a completion: the node asks `explainCompletion` with the shot's take ledger plus the refusal, journals `completionDecision` (`cause: 'render-ceiling'`, the refused code, every recorded take's score), a `cost` row with `disposition: 'refused'`, and a `budget` finding at stage `shoot`, then returns `done`. Under `still-hold` the shoot value is `{ taskId: null, takeId: null, url, lastFrameUrl: url, held: { seconds }, ceiling: { refused, decision } }` where `url` is the promoted keyframe (a cut) or the recorded last frame it would have continued from (a continuous join); `measure` records the planned seconds with no gates, `qc` records no verdict, and `assemble` hands stitch `{ url, seconds }` for that shot so the timeline still sums. Under `promote-best` the value is the best scored take from the ledger with the same `ceiling` marker; `measure` never regenerates a ceiling-marked shot and `qc` reuses the verdict already recorded for that take. A shot with no scored take under `promote-best` is `E-COMPLETION-NO-TAKE`, a fault by name. The card flow knows no ceiling completion: its refusal halts.

### The journal

An in-process single-writer queue with a monotonic counter and fsync. Every paid call is bracketed by an intent before the wire and a result after, so a killed pass resumes without paying twice: `bravo resume <runId>` hydrates `project.json`, verifies it against the journal (the journal wins), keeps done nodes only when their media exists on disk, re-polls a shoot that has a task id and never re-starts it, counts an intent with no task id as a possible orphan against the ceiling, re-issues reasoning and judge calls (tokens, not renders), and re-runs the idempotent local operations. Every reasoning call is journaled with its full system prompt, prompt and answer; every judge answer with its frames, fps, order, stability and falsification; every selection with every candidate's scores; every regeneration with its cause and its number.

Every plan gate report is journaled twice, by two hands. The tool that ran the gates writes a `gate.attempt` step the moment it runs them — `{ tool, phase, attempt, pass, results, blockers, reason }`, one per gate run: the brief's single run, every screenplay attempt, every breakdown structural attempt and the completed plan's full run — through the `journal` the tool context carries; a director tool refuses a context without its journal (`E-TOOL-JOURNAL`) or without the pass rulebook (`E-TOOL-RULEBOOK`), after refusing one without policy values, and never fetches a rulebook of its own. Then `runTool` writes the `gate` step right after the `tool` step whose output carried it: `{ tool, results, blockers, attempts }`. `results` are the full gate rows — rule id, subject, pass, value, threshold, detail, class, blocking, failure kind — as the brief, screenplay and breakdown tools return them in `output.gates` (the brief stage rows; the screenplay's SCR and CIN rows; the completed plan's every row, so SCR-013 has one subject, one force and one change verdict per shot plus the without-force share against the policy ceiling). `attempts` lists every rejected reasoning attempt `{ attempt, blockers, reason }` — the gate rows that refused it and the prose handed back to the reasoner — so a corrected second attempt never erases the first. A tool that refuses after its gates ran returns the same two fields on its error output, and the `gate` step is written before the pass fails.

### The errors database and the five lanes

Every detected error is a `finding` step in the journal first and a row in `errors.ndjson` second. Rows carry a closed taxonomy — family (gate, fault, qc, judge-reliability, waste, budget, drift, shortfall), stage, class, severity, failure kind, code — plus rule, rubric or metric id, shot, join, candidate, take and task ids, timecode, value, threshold, a recurrence signature, evidence pointers, cost, disposition and the correction that answered it. The knowledge index over all passes is derived and deletable.

Harness change happens only through five lanes, each ending in a file that names its rows, all automatic under policy criteria:

- **A · regression** — a blocker finding becomes a learned case in the director suite
- **B · patch** — within a pass: QC findings → regeneration; across passes: the critic patches prompts and plan inputs for the same idea
- **C · proposal** — recurrence of one signature across enough passes and enough distinct ideas invokes the critic; a judgment-class rule lands with provenance; a plan or measure rule enters calibrating and opens a task naming the check it needs (a check is code, and code lands under the test rule)
- **D · calibration** — measured distributions and judge agreement become calibration files; thresholds and rubric authority promote when the policy's criteria are met
- **E · precedent** — winning keyframes and takes with their prompts, scores and verdicts enter the house corpus; the RAG layer plugs in here and at selection

### The creator's channel

`bravo note <runId>` — after a pass, and only after. Notes in the critic's shape, author `human`, filed against the pass. They are the ground truth the judge is calibrated against and the signal lanes B, C and D read. Nothing in a pass waits for them.

## Runtime

A Node CLI, `cli/bravo.js`. Commands: `film`, `resume <runId>`, `judge <runId>` (re-run QC under a new rubric version, no renders), `note <runId>`, `report <runId>`, `kb fold | query | calibrate | rebuild`, `replay <runId> --dry`. The seam is the one the tests already use: a get/apply closure over a plain project object. The CLI synthesizes project → latched director thread → sequence → approved sequence message, calls brief, screenplay and breakdown in fixed order (no model-chosen next step, no turn cap), and hands the sequence to the scheduler. The CLI ships first against a running dev server through a relative-fetch shim; extracting the wire handlers into functions comes last, behind request-shape snapshot tests.

## Style input

```json
{ "id": "", "look": { "style": "", "grade": "" }, "constraints": [], "audio": true, "format": { "resolution": "", "ratio": "" },
  "world": "", "references": [{ "name": "", "role": "character|location|look", "image": "" }],
  "doctrine": [], "precedentTags": [], "seed": null }
```

`look` is required. `format` declares the wire resolution and aspect ratio and `audio` whether sound is generated; together they become `brief.format`, under the frame rate SCR-008 fixes — the brief tool has no format of its own and refuses an input without one. References are checked into the media store at intake as bible entries; character and location references ride as plates into keyframe generation. Look references ride into the judge's look questions and into ideation. Doctrine lines enter the screenplay and breakdown prompts as judgment-class doctrine with provenance, never as gates.

## What is guaranteed

- **A film and a journal, every pass.** Completion is unconditional after intake.
- **Target duration** within the scaled tolerance, enforced by regeneration and downgrade within budget, and reported truthfully when budget runs out.
- **Every shot names its subject, its force and its change** before any money is spent.
- **Nothing reaches the video model unselected**: every take descends from a keyframe that won on evidence.
- **Obvious artifacts do not ship** while the attempt budget lasts; when it does not last, the journal says which take shipped with what.
- **Money is bounded**: no paid call without a reservation; every wasted generation is counted and coded.
- **The judge's authority over story is earned, automatically**, from the creator's post-pass notes; until then it ranks and records.
- **The journal never lies**: every guarantee above appears in `report.md` as met or not met, with the rows.

## Reuse and replace

Reused verbatim: the brief, screenplay and breakdown tools with their gate loops (extended with subject, force, change); the critic; the gates, partition and rulebook loader (extended with the policy class and rubrics); manifestOf and the iteration record; the media store; stitch; the frames route. Replaced: the sequential walk (a scheduler over extracted node bodies; the browser calls it at concurrency 1); human approval (policy); halts on violation (regeneration and named shortfall); fire-and-forget tracing (the journal); localStorage-only persistence (filesystem persistence). Removed: the executor's silent `continuous` default; the resume button; the approval card as a wait.

## Milestones

**M1 · A pass completes on the server shim.** With a dev server up, `bravo film` produces `media/slice-a<admission>.mp4` and a journal for three different ideas with no browser and no wait: ideation with derived-field provenance, the subject/force/change breakdown with SCR-013, policy approval with the arithmetic, disk persistence, the journal as a required in-process writer, the existing sequential executor. Exit: three passes, three complete run directories, every journal kind present, the unit suite green with the policy-class loader test and the SCR-013 gate test.

**M1b · The film as images.** Discovery at four levels with journaled merges, the deduplicated bible with its gates, the library reaching the policy minimum with per-image QC and regeneration, reference selection per shot with its reason, and start frames generated from the selected references. Exit: on a stubbed wire a 20-shot pass journals four discovery levels, a merge, a library of at least the minimum, and a reference set per shot; on the real wire one pass produces a library on disk under `media/library/` with every image's QC verdict in the journal.

**M2 · Candidates, selection, QC, regeneration.** Keyframe candidates per shot, deterministic scoring, the artifact rubric spending from its attempt budget, take QC with regeneration, promotion of the best, completion under an exhausted budget with the journaled downgrade. On the stubbed wire: a clean pass, a shot that regenerates twice and promotes its best, a shot that exhausts attempts and ships with a shortfall, a render ceiling hit mid-pass. Exit: four scenarios green; one real 20-shot pass where every take descends from a scored keyframe and the journal shows every candidate.

**M3 · Chain-parallel scheduler, reservations, resume.** The scheduler over the same nodes; reservations before every paid call; intent/result pairs; resume after a kill without a second task for any shoot that had a task id; invalidation of continuous successors on regeneration. Exit: a 20-shot pass finishing in the expected number of render waves, asserted from timestamps; a killed pass resumed with the cost ledger reconciling.

**M4 · The judge calibrated, the lanes live.** Full rubric sets (plate, take, join, film) with every defense journaled; `bravo note` on five completed passes; M-CALIB per rubric; the first automatic promotion of a story rubric under the policy's criteria; lanes A–D producing files. Exit: a pass whose journal shows a story rubric spending because its calibration rows cleared the bar, and the errors database answering "what recurred, what it cost, what fixed it."

**M5 · In-process runtime and precedent.** No Next server; the wire handlers as functions with snapshot-tested shapes; five passes at three lengths unattended; lane E feeding selection and breakdown from the house corpus. Exit: five unattended passes and the first selection that cites precedent rows.

## Risks

1. The judge is not a director; story rubrics rank and record until calibration earns them authority — early passes complete with open notes, by design.
2. Judge call volume and Seedance queue times make 20–30-shot passes hours long; the wall-minute ceiling will end some passes with downgraded shots, journaled.
3. Keyframe-first video: image-to-video from a still can under-animate; the take QC's frozen-segment check and the change rubric exist for exactly this, and the first passes measure how often it bites.
4. Seedance has no published concurrency limit and no cancel; throttle and timeout rows tune the caps.
5. Orphaned renders between intent and task id are counted, not reconciled, until the provider exposes task listing.
6. Autonomous ideation can make a lawful, dull film; subject/force/change and precedent are the levers, and the creator's post-pass notes are the signal.
7. Identity consistency stays calibrating until an embedding tool exists in the repo — a named milestone item, never a silent dependency.
8. Extracting the wire handlers is the riskiest change to the product the creator uses daily; it lands last.

## Amendments by the creator's go

- **Ceilings are unbounded by default.** A ceiling of `null` in the policy file means no ceiling — declared, journaled, never a default; the pass is bounded by attempt counts, not money.
- **Five attempts everywhere**: plates, keyframe candidates, shots, faults.
- **The harness decides whether a shot needs a start keyframe**, per shot, deterministically, and journals the reason: a cut shot and the first shot need one (identity and setup are anchored by a keyframe generated by Seedream 5.0 Pro from the plates); a continuous shot does not (its first frame is the previous take's recorded last frame; the wire rejects a keyframe alongside references anyway); a continuous shot whose subject or location differs from its predecessor is refused at plan time (SCR-013), not silently re-keyed.
- **Everything is journaled.** Every reasoning call with its full prompts and answer, every plan and gate report, every keyframe decision with its reason, every candidate with its scores, every selection, every render intent and result, every QC verdict with its frames, every regeneration with its cause, every reservation, every fault and retry, every completion decision, the report. A code path that acts without a journal entry is a defect.

## Implementation contracts

Module ownership is strict; a builder edits only the files it owns. Everything below is the interface the other builders code against.

### `agents/journal.js` — owner: journal
```js
openJournal({ dir })                         // creates dir, journal.ndjson, steps/, media/; returns a journal
journal.write(kind, data) -> { step, at }     // appends one NDJSON line + steps/NNNN-kind.json; fsync; monotonic in-process counter
journal.intent(kind, data) -> intentId        // a paid or metered call is about to happen; fsynced before the wire
journal.result(intentId, data)               // its outcome; a result without an intent throws
journal.finding(row)                         // validates against the errors taxonomy, writes a finding step, appends errors.ndjson
journal.cost(row)                            // appends cost.ndjson; a spent row carries the reservationId it spent from
journal.media(name, urlOrBytes) -> path      // downloads or writes into media/, journals the save
journal.entries(kind?) -> array              // read-back for report and resume
journaledClient(journal, client) -> client   // wraps reason/generateImage/startVideo/pollVideo with intent/result pairs and full payloads
```
Every `write` is synchronous from the caller's point of view (a queue with a mutex; the promise resolves after fsync). A failed write throws — in the CLI the journal is required, not best-effort. `intent` returns a Promise that resolves to the id and every caller awaits it before the wire — a node that reads the intent id synchronously holds a Promise, not an id, and the result it files against it refuses; the executor, the schedule and the stages await every `write`, `intent`, `result`, `finding`, `cost` and `media` so a refused row fails the node that wrote it, by name. A cost row's free text is its `detail` column (`COST_SCHEMA` knows no `cause`); the invalidation rows the schedule writes name the regeneration or promotion that discarded the take there. A finding's `runId` is stamped once, at the pass level, by the CLI's `passJournal` adapter — the modules never know it and never invent one — and its `evidence` is always an array of pointers (frame urls, take urls, measurements, intent and reservation ids), never a bare object.

### `agents/director/nodes.js` + `agents/director/schedule.js` — owner: executor (owns `agents/director/execute.js` too)
Node bodies extracted from `execute.js` with identical value shapes, plus new nodes `keyframe:<shot>` and `qc:<shot>`. The `plate:<entity>` node renders on the model the plan names for that plate (`getModel(pl.model)`, at `keyframeImageSize(pl.model)`; a plate without a model is refused by name) and, in the policy flow, hands every render to `stages.plateQC({ plate, style, rubrics, policy, client, journal })`: a failing plate journals `regeneration { entity, attempt, mode: 'plate-rerender', cause }` and re-renders within `policy.attempts.plate`; each attempt is a `plate` record (`entity, attempt, budget, url, pass, score, findings`) and a kept `still` cost row; a pass codes the earlier renders `E-WASTE-QC-REGENERATED`; exhaustion journals `completionDecision { entity, decision: 'promote-best', cause: 'exhausted-plate', attempts, budget, plates }`, files a `shortfall` finding coded `E-PLATE-ATTEMPTS-EXHAUSTED` at stage `plate`, codes the losers `E-WASTE-NOT-PROMOTED` and promotes the best-scoring render. The node value is `{ url, assetId, attempt, pass, score }`; the approved-card flow renders once and has no plate QC. `runSchedule({ manifest, plan, flow, policy, concurrency, nodes, run, complete, journal })` walks the dependency graph (`nodes.get`/`nodes.set` are the run record, `run(id)` a node body, `complete(id, { error, faults })` the completion a node takes when its fault budget is spent — required in the policy flow, refused by name without it): plates depend on nothing; keyframe:i on plates; shoot:i on keyframe:i (cut) or shoot:i-1's last frame (continuous); measure:i on shoot:i; qc:i on measure:i; chain:i on measure:i-1 and measure:i; assemble on every promoted take; final on assemble. Regeneration lives in the shoot/qc pair: a failing take re-shoots within `policy.attempts.shot` (same keyframe first, then a new candidate via candidates.js), invalidating continuous successors; exhaustion journals the completion decision and promotes the best. The qc and measure nodes choose the mode from the shot's counted takes (`regenerateMode(policy, n)`, `n` being the number of takes the ledger counts for the shot, invalidated takes excluded) and return `{ status: 'regenerate', shotId, mode, n, cause, ruleId }`; the schedule refuses a policy-flow qc or measure regeneration without an integer `n` and journals `regeneration { shotId, attempt, n, mode, cause, ruleId, invalidatedShots, reset, inFlight, wasted }`, where `attempt` is the shoot node's admission count and `n` the regeneration number the mode was chosen for. The two differ whenever a shoot faulted and was re-admitted or was invalidated by a predecessor's regeneration: an invalidated successor's first regeneration is its second admission but its first counted take. A keyframe node's `new-candidate` regeneration and a `promote-best` row carry `n: null`. The measure node records every take's measurement and its blocking rule ids on the take ledger (`measure`, `measureBlockers`); a retryable measure blocker (CIN-007) re-shoots within the same budget in the declared `policy.shots.regenerateOrder` (same keyframe first, then a new candidate), exactly as a failing take QC does, and a take that ships over a measure blocker never ships silently: an exhausted retryable rule journals `completionDecision { decision: policy.completion.onExhaustedShot, cause: 'exhausted-shot', ruleId, attempts, budget, takes }` and promotes the measured take closest to its planned seconds (latest attempt on a tie), a deterministic rule (CIN-004) journals `completionDecision { decision: 'accept-measured-blocker', cause: 'deterministic-measure-blocker', ... }` and keeps the current take; both file a `shortfall` finding coded `E-SHOT-MEASURE-SHIPPED` at stage `measure` carrying every measured attempt, and the measure node's value describes the promoted take. A start the provider refuses on its output-audio policy while the brief declares audio is a downgrade, never a silent one: before the retake without audio the shoot node files a `shortfall` finding coded `E-AUDIO-POLICY-SILENT` at stage `shoot` (severity `note`, disposition `downgrade`, evidence naming the refused `render.start` intent and the reservation) , writes a `cost` row with `disposition: 'refused'` coded `E-AUDIO-POLICY` (kind `take`, naming the refused intent and the reservation) and journals `regeneration { mode: 'silent-retake', cause, attempt, intentId, reservationId, justification }`; the refused start produced no task and spent nothing, so the retake spends the same reservation and the justification says so. A plate is QC'd before anything descends from it: in the policy flow the plate node reserves, renders and journals each attempt (`plate-<entity>-a<admission>-attempt<n>` media — the admission is the schedule's run count for the node, so a resumed or re-admitted plate never meets the journal's refusal to overwrite — and a kept `still` cost row carrying the attempt), hands it to `stages.plateQC`, and journals a `plate` step with the verdict; a failing plate journals `regeneration { entity, attempt, mode: 'plate-rerender', cause }` and renders again within `policy.attempts.plate`, and once a later attempt passes every rejected render is re-coded `E-WASTE-QC-REGENERATED`; on exhaustion it journals `completionDecision { entity, decision: 'promote-best', cause: 'exhausted-plate', attempts, budget, plates }`, files a `shortfall` finding coded `E-PLATE-ATTEMPTS-EXHAUSTED` at stage `plate`, codes the losers `E-WASTE-NOT-PROMOTED` and promotes the best score. The kept plate is journaled once more as `plate-<entity>` media and enters the bible; the node's value is `{ url, assetId, attempt, pass, score }`. The approved-card flow carries no judge, so its plate node journals the same `plate` step with `pass: null` and a `skipped` reason instead of a verdict. The browser's `runSequence` calls the same scheduler at concurrency 1. No node halts the pass after intake; every violation is a finding.

**Faults, backoff, completion and the one halt.** A node that throws is a fault: the schedule journals `fault { id, attempt, faults, budget, reason, backoffMs }`, and in the policy flow re-admits the node after `policy.resume.backoffMs` (a `waiting` timer beside the running set; nothing else is held back) until `policy.attempts.fault` is spent. Exhaustion journals `schedule { event: 'exhausted' }` and hands the node to `complete(id, { error, faults })`; the completion's outcome stands in for the node and the pass goes on. The executor's `completeNode` completes a `keyframe` from its scored candidates (`E-KEYFRAME-FAULTS-EXHAUSTED`), a `shoot` from its recorded takes through `explainCompletion` (`E-SHOT-FAULTS-EXHAUSTED`), and a `qc` by shipping the current take unjudged: `completionDecision { decision: 'ship-unjudged', cause: 'exhausted-faults', faulted, faults, takes }`, a `qc` step with `pass: null`, and a `fault` finding coded `E-QC-FAULTS-EXHAUSTED` at stage `qc`. A completion that refuses by name — a plate, measure, chain, assemble or final node with nothing to complete from (`E-COMPLETION-<KIND>`), or a shoot with no recorded take (`E-COMPLETION-NO-TAKE`) — is the one halt the policy flow knows: `schedule { event: 'completion-failed' }`, then `{ status: 'halted', halted: { node, reason } }` where the reason names the fault, the count against the budget and the refusal, and the run record keeps the node's value (a task id survives for resume). The approved-card flow is the explicit halting mode: it carries no policy, retries nothing and halts at the first fault or the first measure blocker; a node that asks to halt in the policy flow is a defect and throws.

**Admissions and attempts.** The schedule's `attempts` on a node is its admission count — the number the media names carry (`shoot-<shot>-attempt<n>`, `keyframe-<shot>-attempt<n>`, `<shot>-a<n>-c<i>`), so every run writes fresh evidence. The budget the policy bounds is counted on the take ledger: the measure and qc nodes count the shot's takes that the schedule has not invalidated (`shoot` node `invalidated`), so a successor take discarded because its predecessor regenerated — finished or in flight — is coded `E-WASTE-INVALIDATED-SUCCESSOR` and is not one of the shot's attempts. Exhaustion in `qc` runs through `explainCompletion` over the counted takes, so a take without a verdict is never compared as a score and a shot with no scored take is `E-COMPLETION-NO-TAKE` by name; the decision carries `cause: 'exhausted-shot'`.

**Policy rules run where their statements say, and the plan regenerates under them.** Every policy rule after intake is journaled where it runs: `policy.gate { ruleId, stage, pass, results, blockers }` for POL-001 (on the ideation's provenance, inside the ideate stage, so a refusal is a fault the stage retries), POL-012 and POL-006 (on the plan, stage `keyframes`) and POL-002 (stage `approve`); `policy.audit { scope: 'journal' }` at completion for POL-003, POL-004, POL-005, POL-007, POL-008, POL-011 and POL-013, read from the journal alone (POL-003 pairs every kept `cost` row's `reservationId` with a `reservation` step that preceded it; POL-005 reads every `regeneration` row whose mode is in the declared order and whose `n` is an integer >= 1, and recomputes the mode from `n`, never from the admission `attempt`); and `policy.audit { scope: 'knowledge' }` after every fold for POL-009 and POL-010 over the folded index. A blocker in an audit is a `gate` finding coded `E-POLICY-<RULE>`. A plan that fails POL-006 or POL-012 is not a failed pass: the CLI journals `regeneration { stage: 'plan', mode: 'plan-regenerate', attempt, budget, cause, ruleId, blockers }` and runs the breakdown again with the refusal in its prompt (`input.rejected`, the gate rows in prose, which the tool validates as a non-empty list of strings), within `policy.attempts.fault`; each attempt writes its own `plan` and `keyframes` steps carrying `attempt` and `budget`, and exhaustion fails the pass by name (`E-PLAN-POLICY-EXHAUSTED`) before any money. `report.md` lists every rule run, where, and whether it held, right after the guarantees.

**The run record is on disk while the schedule runs.** The CLI's `apply` writes `project.json` through after every change the schedule makes (an atomic write, coalesced while one is in flight, flushed before the pass goes on; a write that failed refuses by name, `E-PROJECT-WRITE`), so a killed pass leaves a run record `bravo resume` can hydrate, and `report.md` is written last — after the fold and the knowledge audit — so the report the pass writes is the report `bravo report` regenerates.

**Poll and render settings.** The shoot node polls with `ctx.poll = { timeoutMs, maxMs }`: `timeoutMs` is one poll's wait, `maxMs` the most a render may take before the node faults by name. The policy flow reads them from `policy.poll`; the browser passes its declared `BROWSER_POLL` beside `BROWSER_CONCURRENCY`; a context without them is refused at entry. `manifestOf(seq, rulebook)` reads the timeline tolerance from CIN-008's `params.base` and `params.perShot` and the render parameters (`fps, resolution, ratio, audio`) from `brief.format`, each refused by name when absent — a format the `brief` tool recorded from its caller's `format: { resolution, ratio, audio }` (refused by name when missing, malformed, or carrying an fps of its own) under the fps SCR-008's `params.fps` declares (`E-TOOL-FPS` when the law declares none); a shot's `join` is recorded as declared (`null` when none), never defaulted, and CIN-003 refuses a join without a declared type. A pass carries one loaded rulebook: `pass.rulebook` is required and threaded through the context, the director tools take it from `ctx.rulebook` (so the sequence pins the same `rulebookVersion` intake recorded, and resume's version check is against one law), and the `sequence` tool's `prepare` takes it as `rulebook`; only the browser's approved-card flow fetches `/api/rules`, and there `prepare` falls back to the rulebook that fetch loaded (`loadedRulebook()`, refused by name when none is).

### Integration corrections — the seams the cross-builder test found, now law

Each of these was a place where two builders coded to different readings of the contract above; the contract now says one thing and the pass suite (`tests/lib/pass.test.js`) pins it.

- **Evidence is an array.** `FINDING_SCHEMA.evidence` is `array`; every finding the QC modules file wraps its pointers in a list, and `runId` is stamped by the pass (`passJournal`), not by the module.
- **Intent is asynchronous.** `journal.intent()` returns a Promise; every node awaits it before the wire, and `result()` takes the resolved id.
- **Reservations are awaited before every paid call.** `reserve()` is async; `plate`, `keyframe` (through `generateCandidates`) and `shoot` await it before the intent is journaled, so a refusal is a rejected promise the node observes, never an unhandled rejection with a render proceeding behind it.
- **Ids and media names carry the attempt.** Candidate ids are `<shot>-a<attempt>-c<i>` and media names derive from them; plates are `plate-<entity>-a<admission>-attempt<n>`; takes and keyframes `-attempt<n>`; the film is `slice-a<admission>.mp4` and the `assemble` step carries `media` and `admission`, which `complete`, the report and resume's expected-media check read instead of a fixed name. A regeneration writes fresh evidence beside the earlier attempt's instead of meeting `journal.media`'s refusal to overwrite.
- **Every paid unit is a cost row naming its reservation.** Keyframe candidates, like plates and takes, are reserved, written as kept `still` rows carrying `reservationId`, `shotId`, `candidateId` and `attempt`, and counted in the run's `spentRenders`; a wasted row's free text is `detail`.
- **Siblings are the pool, passed once.** The keyframe node hands `scoreCandidate` the shot's whole candidate pool as `siblings`; the CLI hands the candidate stages through untouched and remembers no batch of its own.
- **Still-hold is built.** A `shoot` reservation refused with an `E-BUDGET-` code completes under `completionDecision` as described in "Completion when budget runs out": the held still, the planned seconds, the coded `budget` finding, the refused cost row, and `{ url, seconds }` entries that stitch and measure accept so the timeline still sums.
